# 03. Past Winners

Each year NASA picks **10 Global Winners**, one per award (Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Use of Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact). 2020 had 7 winners; before 2021 the award set differed.

Sources: NASA announcements and the official site (see [05-sources-and-caveats.md](05-sources-and-caveats.md)). Where a source did not name the challenge, the note says so.

## Pattern across 2021-2025 winners (interpretation)

- Winners come from many countries every year. Brazil, India, Egypt, Taiwan, Ukraine, the United States and Bangladesh recur.
- Most winning projects have a **clear audience and a working demo**: dashboards, web apps, games, comics, stories.
- Education and storytelling awards often go to games, comics and children's content (2023 comic book, 2025 illustrated children's story, 2025 farming mini-games).
- Big-data and model projects win Data and Technology awards (2023 LSTM storm forecasting, 2024 neural-network quake detection, 2025 exoplanet AI).
- Thailand: no Thai team appears among winners 2021-2025. A Bangkok team (TheAISmart) was a Global Nominee in 2022.

## 2025 (Theme "Learn, Launch, Lead")

114,094 registered participants, 551 local events, 167 countries and territories, 11,511 projects, 18 challenges, 14 Space Agency Partners. About 1,290 Global Nominees, 45 Global Finalists (named 26 Nov 2025), 23 Honorable Mentions, 10 Global Winners (announced 18 Dec 2025).

| Award | Team | Location | Project |
|-------|------|----------|---------|
| Best Use of Science | SpaceGenes+ | Germany | Interactive dashboard showing how radiation and low gravity together affect astronauts at the molecular level, to support safer long-duration spaceflight planning |
| Best Use of Data | Resonant Exoplanets | United States | AI system that processes large telescope and satellite datasets, including James Webb spectra, to search for exoplanets and flag possible biosignatures |
| Best Use of Technology | Twisters | Mexico | SkySense: web app combining NASA Earth-observing data with AI for hyperlocal weather forecasts and safest-time recommendations for outdoor activities |
| Galactic Impact | Astro Sweepers: We Catch What Space Leaves Behind | Universal Event | Platform that automatically processes public orbital data into debris assessment reports and a risk score per tracked object (challenge: Commercializing Low Earth Orbit) |
| Best Mission Concept | PureFlow | Brazil | Systems-engineering platform to design and model space habitats in 3D and test them against space-weather hazards (waste, power, life support, communications) |
| Most Inspirational | Photonics Odyssey | India (Chennai) | Proposal to treat satellite internet as national infrastructure, with a phased-array antenna design to reach the 700 million+ people without broadband |
| Best Use of Storytelling | HerCode Space | Universal Event | "A Solar Tale": illustrated children's story using NASA data and heliophysics to explain how space weather affects daily life |
| Global Connection | Gaia+LEO | United States (Seattle) | Optimization framework co-designing orbital and ground data-center networks for large-scale AI training and climate modeling, cutting Earth-based energy and water demand |
| Art & Technology | Zumorroda-X | Egypt | Mini-games where players farm around the world and learn how farmers adapt to heat waves, floods and other conditions |
| Local Impact | QUEÑARIS | Peru (Arequipa) | Platform tackling water scarcity from lost queñua forests using native microbes, NASA satellite data, drones and AI to speed tree growth and pick reforestation sites |

Some outlets credited Photonics Odyssey with a different honor. NASA's announcement names it Most Inspirational.
