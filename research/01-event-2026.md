# 01. Event Details: 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge

Primary source: official site (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/, Participant FAQs updated 2026-11-05). Raw copies are in `raw/2026-event/`.

## Overview

- **Name:** 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge (NASA International Space Apps Challenge).
- **Theme:** "The Next Frontier". Official wording: "From your own backyard to the edges of the universe, discovery awaits at the NASA Space Apps Challenge. This is your opportunity to collaborate with a community of like-minded problem solvers to turn data into tools that benefit humanity and propel us to the Moon, Mars, and beyond."
- **Format:** two-day hackathon, Saturday 14 and Sunday 15 November 2026, at hundreds of in-person or virtual Local Events, plus the virtual asynchronous Universal Event.
- **Organizer and funding:** funded by NASA's Science Mission Directorate (SMD) through a contract with Booz Allen Hamilton, Mindgrub and SecondMuse. The program sits under the SMD Earth Science Division.
- **NASA statement (science.nasa.gov, 2026-10-15):** Nicky Fox, associate administrator of SMD: "At NASA, science is for everyone" and an invitation to "build the ultimate interplanetary survival guide". NASA states 500+ local events and that programming skills are not required.

## How to participate (official steps)

1. Create or log in to a NASA Space Apps account at spaceappschallenge.org (email verification).
2. Register for the 2026 hackathon (required to be eligible for Global Judging). Open until the hackathon closes on 15 Nov.
3. Choose a Local Event (in-person or virtual), or the virtual Universal Event if none is near. Changing events is allowed until 15 Nov. Full events offer a waitlist.
4. Form or join a team around one challenge (team formation opened 17 Sep).
5. Work during 14-15 Nov. **Teams may not start work on challenges before the hackathon.**
6. Submit the project on the team's Project tab. A submission earns a participant certificate and Global Judging eligibility.
7. Complete the participant survey (1 Dec).

## Rules and eligibility

- **Who:** any age, skill level or background. No cost. If anyone asks for payment, the organizers say do not pay and report it to info@spaceappschallenge.org.
- **Under 18:** must be registered by a parent or legal guardian and accompanied by one at all times during the event. Local events may add requirements. Under-18s must not upload their own photo as an avatar or team photo, and must not use photo, video or voice of themselves or other minors in the project demo or submission.
- **Teams:** at most 6 members, all registered for the same Local Event or the Universal Event. One team per person, one challenge per team. A Local Event team needs at least 1 person and a Universal Event team at least 2 to be eligible for global judging.
- **Universal Event:** judged in the same period, but Universal Event projects compete only against other Universal Event projects. Organizers say the selection rate for Global Nominees is comparable to local events.
- **"Super Intelligence" (SI) tools:** the FAQ says that, following a 29 September 2026 U.S. Executive Order, the executive branch uses "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI". Participants may use SI tools (large language models, assistants, image and video generators, foundation models, code generation tools). **Each submission must describe how SI tools were used** (details in the submission section below). The executive order itself was not independently verified in this research; this is what the official FAQ states.
- **Conduct:** harassment reports go to the Local Lead or conduct@spaceappschallenge.org.

### Project submission requirements (official Project Submission Guide, dated 12 Nov 2026)

- **Window:** opens Sat 14 Nov 9:00 AM and closes Sun 15 Nov 11:59 PM, in the time zone of the team's Local Event (Universal Event: the Team Owner's local time). The **Project** tab on the team page appears only when the hackathon starts. Local events may run local judging and awards on different times from the global deadline.
- **Editing:** use "Save Progress" often; changes stay private to the team until you click **Submit for Judging**. An edit "Lock" toggle warns teammates, but another member can remove it, so coordinate. You can edit and resubmit until the end of the hackathon. **Only the last saved submission is reviewed.** Anything saved or submitted after the deadline is not judged.
- **Form fields:** Project Name; High-Level Project Summary; Link to Project Demo; Link to Final Project; Detailed Project Description (what it does, how it works, benefits, goals, tools and languages used); Use of Super Intelligence (SI); NASA Data; Space Agency Partner Data and Other References.
- **Files and links:** the page accepts pasted or uploaded images only (JPG, PNG, GIF up to 10 MB). **Videos and documents cannot be uploaded.** Host them on YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox or similar and give a public link that needs no login, permission or registration. If judges cannot open it, it cannot be judged. Test the link in a private window. For a newly built website, the guide suggests requesting a FortiGuard Web Filter Classification (category of your choice; company name "N/A") so judges' networks do not block it. This is optional.
- **Demo content:** a 30-second video outline is suggested (0:00 name and team, 0:02 challenge, 0:05 project title and tagline, 0:10 how it works and which NASA or partner data, 0:20-0:30 vision). A slide demo should show an image of the project, the user experience, and how NASA and space-agency data make it possible, and state the team name, member names and the chosen challenge. The demo counts toward the overall score, but most of the evaluation is based on the full submission.
- **NASA data:** to be eligible for a Global Award a project must use **at least one NASA data set or resource**. Space Agency Partner data is encouraged.
- **SI disclosure:** the SI question is for data collection and judges do not see the answer, but judges do see SI indicators inside the project. SI tools are allowed to accelerate work, but "should not be used to come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge". SI-generated content must not use or modify NASA branding (logos, flags, mission identifiers). Images and videos need a **visible watermark** marking SI generation. Audio, code and data must acknowledge SI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
- **People under 18:** no images, video, audio or likeness of anyone under 18 in the demo or submission.
- **Waitlist trap:** every team member must be confirmed at the same Local Event. If waitlisted, ask the Local Lead for acceptance, or move the whole team to another event with space.
- **Certificate:** after a successful on-time submission, download it from Profile, Participant Information, "Download Participant Certificate" (PDF, Letter size; best from a desktop). Your Full Name in the profile is what prints. Problems go to web@spaceappschallenge.org.

### Participant Terms & Conditions (updated 7 Aug 2026; key points)

Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/ . This is a summary, not legal advice; read the full text before registering.

- **Voluntary, unpaid:** participants are not employees or contractors of NASA or the U.S. government and are not compensated. People who work for a U.S. federal contractor must not cite that affiliation.
- **Start date:** events start 14 Nov 2026 (or another day that week approved in writing by the Global Organizing Team).
- **China restriction:** NASA policy limits cooperation with China, including Hong Kong and Macau. The terms say participants **may not participate in connection with an affiliation with China or a Chinese-owned entity** (including universities), and may not collaborate or coordinate with such entities for the event. It is based on affiliation, not nationality.
- **Solutions must address a challenge statement** to be posted. Content is rejected if it contains threats, hate speech, sexual material, commercial promotion, proprietary or business-sensitive data, material you do not own, or personally identifiable information. No names, voices or likenesses of under-18s.
- **Open licensing:** original content must be freely available or under an OSI-approved open-source license. If you name no license, NASA distributes it under **Apache 2.0**. Anyone can download and use posted solutions.
- **Third-party materials:** datasets or code that do not come from NASA or its agency partners must be freely and publicly available, or open-source licensed. The submission must list the licenses, cite every source, and not contain export-controlled information (ITAR/EAR). Submissions without license documentation are not accepted or posted.
- **Publicity and media release:** submitting gives NASA and Local Leads permission to use your name, country, likeness and project info in any media, worldwide, with no time limit and no compensation. Video submissions must be your original work; the U.S. government gets a royalty-free, perpetual license to use them.
- **Liability:** in-person attendance is at your own risk, and you release NASA from claims. Each local event is solely responsible for complying with local prize or contest laws.
- **Winners Celebration:** defined as a visit by global winners to a NASA Center or facility for an award presentation.
- **Space Apps Connect:** free optional forum for participants, Local Leads, Navigators, subject matter experts (SMEs), collaborators and the organizing team; open 3 Nov through the end of the hackathon.

## Judging and awards

**Process (official):**

1. Local Event (or Universal Event) judges review submissions and choose Global Nominees. In 2025 about 1,290 of 11,511 projects advanced.
2. Global judges (experts from NASA, Space Agency Partners and industry) pick Global Finalists (45 in 2025) and Honorable Mentions (23 in 2025).
3. An executive committee of NASA and Space Agency Partner SMEs picks the 10 Global Winners.

**Criteria (official Judging & Awards Guide, dated 12 Nov 2026).** Every judging level, from local events to executive judges, scores each project **1 to 5** on five criteria:

| Criterion | What it rewards |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Impact | Addresses a minor or major problem; reach and how much it inspires or helps others |
| Creativity | Originality; a new concept or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas |
| Validity | Scientifically grounded and technically, functionally or logically feasible; usable and complete |
| Relevance | Directly addresses the chosen challenge and uses NASA data as a core component, not a superficial add-on |
| Presentation | Clear communication of the challenge, solution and significance; judges score the demo and the full project |

The guide tells teams to give Presentation equal attention to the first four, to recruit storytelling and design skills, and to invest in the demo.

**Phases and dates (official):**

1. **Local judging and Global Nominees.** Local judges (recruited by each Local Lead) pick nominees. Universal Event projects are judged by subject matter experts, and the number advancing is proportional to the local-event share. Only officially submitted Global Nominees reach global judging, whatever local awards were given. Nominees announced **3 Dec 2026**.
2. **Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions.** Expert judges score all nominees on the criteria. Finalists and Honorable Mentions announced **17 Dec 2026**. Honorable Mentions ranked highly but do not advance to executive judging or the awards.
3. **Global Winners.** An executive committee from NASA and the 2026 Space Agency Partners reviews each finalist and nominates teams for the ten awards. Winners announced **26 Jan 2027**.

The guide stresses that Space Apps is "a collaborative event, not a competitive one". Everyone who submits on time gets a certificate.

**Eligibility for Global Judging (minimum requirements from the guide):**

- The project responds to one of the 14 official challenges.
- Every team member is registered, in the same Local Event (not waitlisted) or the Universal Event, and listed on the team's Members tab. No members can be added after submission closes. Teams have at most 6 members; at least 1 (local) or 2 (Universal Event).
- All required fields on the Project Submission page are complete.
- A **demo**: either a slide deck of **at most 7 slides** (title and intro included) or a video of **at most 30 seconds**. Projects exceeding these limits are not eligible for global judging.
- English on the submission page and linked materials. A video demo may be spoken in another language only with English subtitles.
- Submitted before Sunday 15 Nov, 11:59 PM local time of the team's Local Event (Universal Event: the Team Owner's local time).
- Both checkboxes ticked: the Terms and Conditions agreement, and the statement that the work is original and every external resource (code, text, images, even open-source ones) is listed in the References field, with no copyrighted material used without permission.
- A review for prohibited bilateral activity with China or a Chinese-owned entity (defined to include Hong Kong and Macau, Chinese government bodies, Chinese-owned companies, and universities or institutions incorporated in China). NASA decides eligibility in its sole discretion, based on information the applicant provides.

**The 10 Global Awards** (official awards page):

| Award | Definition |
|-------|------------|
| Best Use of Science | Best and most valid use of science and/or the scientific method |
| Best Use of Data | Best makes space data accessible or leverages it for a unique application |
| Best Use of Technology | Most innovative use of technology |
| Galactic Impact | Most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe |
| Best Mission Concept | Most plausible concept and design |
| Most Inspirational | "The project that captures our hearts" |
| Best Use of Storytelling | Most creative communication of open data through storytelling |
| Global Connection | Best connects people around the world through technology |
| Art & Technology | Most effective combination of technical and creative skills |
| Local Impact | The project that demonstrates the greatest potential for making a difference in their local community |

**Recognition and prizes:**

- Every participant who submits gets a certificate on their profile page.
- No cash prize is documented for global winners. The FAQ says details about 2026 Global Winner recognition "will be announced at a later date".
- Past global winners (2023, 2024, 2025) were invited to in-person celebrations at NASA Headquarters (Washington, DC) and NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (Greenbelt, MD). Earlier winners (2012-2018) were invited to NASA launches.
- Local events set their own prizes. Examples: Alba Iulia, Romania, EUR 6,000 total (3,000 / 2,000 / 1,000); Kamloops, Canada, three local prizes with a sponsor prize for first place.
