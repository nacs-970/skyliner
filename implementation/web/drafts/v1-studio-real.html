First time here? This page plays real NASA SPHEREx infrared images of one patch of sky, in time order, so you can look for something that moves. Press Space to play, or drag along the timeline. The gaps in the timeline are real: SPHEREx looks at a position in windows that are months apart. It is a viewer only. It does not detect or name moving objects.

The faint targets (WISE 0855 and Epsilon Indi B) cannot be seen in a single frame. Barnard’s Star is bright and moves about 1.7 pixels a year, so it is a good first look.

A detector marked not prepared yet is read from NASA's archive by the local service when you choose it. That takes a few minutes, with a progress bar.

One detector plays at a time, because frames of different detectors differ in wavelength, not only in time. Even within one detector the wavelength at the target changes from frame to frame (see Frame details), so a brightness change can be spectral and not variability. The cache option shows the same frames decoded from the lossy cell store (about 10 to 15 times smaller); it is for quick looks, not measurements. Each frame has its own background level removed for display.

For testing the controls only, not a detection. Every frame is the same real crop plus random noise that changes smoothly from frame to frame (the same frame always looks the same), a slow background drift and optional sub-pixel jitter. Set noise, drift and jitter to 0 and hide the mover to see the pure real crop. A real mover such as WISE 0855 would shift only about 1 pixel over the whole baseline (set the mover speed to 1).
